01 / Opening popularity

Most vs. Least Popular Opening Moves

Definition: The first moves by either side in a chess game. Common opening moves have standard names, such as the "Sicilian Defense".

Data Organization: The original dataset had over {{ "{:,}".format(19000) }} unique opening moves, including variations of standard openings. We narrowed this down to 18 umbrella openings, and 1 fallback category ("Other").

{{ plot('opening-frequency', 'Horizontal bar chart of the ten most frequent opening families. Other is largest, followed by Sicilian Defense.', 'Figure 1: Frequency of the top 10 opening families.') }}

Key Takeaway: The most frequent opening moves fall into the "Other" category, reflecting a lot of variation in opening moves. The "Sicilian Defense" is the second most frequent opening, beating the third opening by over 1,200 games.

Chess.com describes the "Sicilian Defense" as a "employed by masters and beginners alike... a reputable and positionally sound opening... that tends to lead to dynamic and sharp positions." Assumedly, this opening is popular for its flexibility.

On the other hand, Chess.com describes the "English Opening" as a move most suitable for intermediate to advanced players with a deeper understanding of piece positioning, hence explaining its unpopularity.

02 / Overall results

Does White win more often?

Definition: In Chess, the White side plays the first move.

Figure 2 is a pie chart comparison between games won by Black vs. White. Drawn games are not included in the calculation.

{% set wins = analysis.win_rates %}

Z-Test Set-up: Let p = the proportion of games won by White, and alpha (significance level) = 0.05.

Set up the Null Hypothesis: White and Black have an equal chance of winning; p = 0.5

Set up the Alternative Hypothesis: White has a higher chance of winning than Black; p > 0.5

Assume that the set of games is randomly sampled, each game is independent, and the games have a normal approximation distribution.
